The Eight Little Ideas
Inside Every Big AI

Chatbots that write stories. Apps that draw pictures. They all run on a few small ideas you can play with right now: patterns, numbers, mixing, and clever guessing. Collect all eight ideas to see how the whole machine fits together!

AI is a pattern-spotting machine

Big idea · patterns

Before anything else, AI is about finding patterns, just like you do. When you guess what comes next in a song, a dance, or a row of shapes, your brain uses the past to predict the future. That is exactly the trick every AI is built on.

2

Computers see pictures as grids of numbers

Big idea · data

A computer has no eyes. A picture, to a computer, is just a grid of numbers: 1 where the ink is, 0 where it isn't. (Real photos use bigger numbers for brightness and colour, but the idea is the same.) Paint on the grid and watch the numbers change instantly.

🎨 Pixel painter · draw on the left, read the numbers on the right

Try drawing a letter or a smiley. The number grid is your drawing. That's all the computer ever sees!

🤖Robo says: When an AI "looks" at a photo, it is really reading a giant grid of numbers, millions of them. Spotting a cat means spotting a pattern in the numbers. Idea 1 + Idea 2 together!
3

Words become numbers too

Big idea · data

Chatbots can't read letters either! Every word gets swapped for numbers before the AI sees it. A simple code like A=1, B=2 works for letters; real AIs give each word a whole list of numbers that describes its meaning, so similar words get similar lists.

4

The mixing machine: multiply, then add

Big idea · weighted sums

Here is the single most important sum in all of AI: multiply each number by how much it matters, then add everything up. Cooks do it when balancing flavours; judges do it when scoring a contest. Inside an AI, this little "mixing machine" is called a neuron, and big AIs stack up billions of them.

🥤 The smoothie judge · will Robo like your smoothie?

🤖Robo says: The purple sliders are my weights: they decide what matters to me. A negative weight means "the more of this, the worse!". Learning, as you'll see in Idea 6, is just finding the right weights.
5

The decision lamp: only strong signals pass

Big idea · activation

After mixing, a neuron makes a small decision: is the score big enough to bother telling the next neuron? If yes, the signal passes; if not, it stays quiet. This on/off gate is what lets a pile of simple sums make genuinely clever choices.

💡
🤖Robo says: Scientists call this an activation function. My favourite one is beautifully lazy: "if the score is negative, say 0; otherwise pass it along." Simple gates + simple sums, stacked in layers = me!

Learning = shrinking your mistakes

Big idea · training

Nobody types the right weights into an AI: it finds them itself, the same way you learn darts: throw, see how far off you were, adjust a little, throw again. Every wrong answer tells the machine which way to nudge its weights. Watch it happen live.

7

AI answers with chances, not certainty

Big idea · probability

An AI never says "the answer IS cat." It says "80% cat, 15% fox, 5% dog", a list of chances. Then it picks. Usually it picks the most likely one, but not always: a little randomness is what makes chatbots say something new each time.

8

Chatbots write one word at a time

Big idea · generation

Now stack every idea together. A chatbot reads your words (as numbers), mixes them through millions of neurons, and produces chances for the next word. It picks one, adds it to the sentence, and repeats, one word at a time, until the story is done. You be the chatbot: build a sentence by picking from the machine's suggestions.
